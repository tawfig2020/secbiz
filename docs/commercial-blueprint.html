Executive Summary & Problem Space

The $120B Autonomous Banking Transition

Global financial institutions are racing to deploy autonomous AI agents to automate high-volume operations: invoice settlement, SWIFT messaging, trade reconciliation, customer underwriting, and wealth advisory. By 2028, over 65% of Tier-1 banks will rely on autonomous multi-agent pipelines to execute transactions.

The Critical Industry Vulnerability

Large Language Models are probabilistic text engines. According to rigorous empirical benchmarks by NIST and OWASP, frontier LLMs remain vulnerable to Prompt Injection (OWASP LLM #1) and confused-deputy attacks ~81% of the time. When an autonomous banking agent reads an untrusted PDF invoice containing an invisible injection payload, it becomes hijacked.

Why Incumbent Guardrails Fail

Traditional solutions (e.g., system prompts, Python wrappers, output classifiers) operate inside the model boundary. They effectively ask a compromised model to police itself. If the core reasoning layer is deceived, internal guardrails collapse.

The AEGIS-BFSI Paradigm Shift

AEGIS-BFSI enforces safety strictly outside the model. By splitting agents into untrusted Readers and tool-restricted Executors governed by formal, signed Capability Contracts, a hijacked model cannot move money. Containment is deterministic and mathematically proven.
